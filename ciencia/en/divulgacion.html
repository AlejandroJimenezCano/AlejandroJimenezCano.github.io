Black holes in relativity: how do they work? (Semana de la Ciencia)

This outreach talk aims to convey an accurate and rigorous picture of how a black hole works, as described by General Relativity (A. Einstein). It introduces the basic concepts of relativity neede (spacetime diagrams, coordinates and light cones) to visualise, on something like a "map of the black hole", what happens to observers who fall beyond the horizon, both from an outside observer’s perspective and from their own. There is a significant discrepancy between the "intuitive" black hole typically portrayed in science fiction and the somewhat stranger one that emerges from Einstein’s equations. The talk addresses this discrepancy. Numerical examples are also provided to help build intuition about the scales of the phenomena discussed.

Discovering gravity (Aula científica permanente de la UGR)

When we release an object, it falls, but how? We are not used to asking questions like this because falling objects are part of everyday life. Yet the subject has its subtleties: What pulls objects towards the ground? Does it have anything in common with magnets? What determines how fast objects fall? Why do astronauts float?

A talk featuring small experiments and audiovisual material to illustrate some of gravity’s most intriguing aspects. Alongside sharing scientific knowledge, one of its main aims is to encourage students to ask questions and answer them using the scientific method.

Where are the electrons? (Aula científica permanente de la UGR)

Where are the electrons? What do they do? Do they orbit the nucleus? Nothing could be further from the truth! And what’s that? You’ve heard about a cat that is alive and dead at the same time? That last part sounds fishy.

The quantum world defies common sense and forces us to think in unfamiliar ways that challenge our intuition. In this talk, I offer a few glimpses of this way of thinking, aiming to help us better understand how electrons behave inside atoms.
